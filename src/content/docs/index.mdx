Agentic systems need agents to solve tasks, models to make decisions, and data to ground them. Each topic takes one of them and explains what a large organization sets up once for many teams to run it.

The guide covers agents that do business work: assistants that people work with, such as one that drafts replies to customers, and agents that run on their own inside business processes, such as one that checks each new invoice against its order. Agents across the software development lifecycle (SDLC), from writing code to testing and releasing it, go through the same controls, but their specifics stay out of the guide.

A large organization has to control both where its data goes and what its agents do, because an agentic system sends the data to whoever runs each part and acts on what comes back. When a vendor runs a part, its **data terms** decide what happens to the data: whether it is used for training, how long it is kept, and in which region it is processed. Enterprise agreements usually exclude training and limit retention, and a regional deployment can meet **data residency**, the need to keep data in a given country. Where the terms are enough, the vendor's solution is simpler and usually the better choice. Where they fall short, the organization has to own that part, and only for the work that needs it, because it takes on the cost and the expertise that a vendor would otherwise bring. Either way, it has to govern its agents, because data terms do not limit what agents do.
